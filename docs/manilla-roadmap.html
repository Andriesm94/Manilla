Build roadmap as of 2026-09-04

Manilla Roadmap

Eight milestones from a blank folder to a competing fleet of AI traders. Six are shipped, including a REV agent that bids, buys, loads, and places on its own — now with its seat values measured from self-play rather than guessed. A learned share-buying model is trained, wired in, and beats the heuristic it replaced by +3.4 pesos a voyage. What is left is comparing the whole agent against random at scale.

3play

Full playable voyage loop

● Shipped

Not in the original plan as its own milestone — this is what milestones 4–7 of the original rules-engine plan collapsed into once real gameplay requests started arriving. Covers the entire rulebook flow for a live voyage:

  • Locked new-game setup, bid/pass auction, harbor-master share purchase, ware loading & punt placement
  • Turn-based accomplice placement with automatic payment and slot-priority rules across every space type
  • Automatic dice rolls, arrivals, shipwrecks, pirate boarding & plunder, pilot movement
  • Full profit distribution, black-market rises, a credit/blind-passenger system so cash never goes negative
  • End-of-game ranked standings with correct share-unencumbering-under-scarcity math
  • Random-policy computer players for any or all seats, chained through a full unattended game
  • Hand-dealt setup (2026-09-04): state the opening deal instead of shuffling it, for reproducing a position from a real table

Hand-dealt setup, 2026-09-04. A computer seat says what it was dealt; a human seat doesn't have to, and its two shares are held unrecorded — counted, with no ware attached. That makes the stock numbers load-bearing rather than a convenience: every share neither dealt out nor for sale has to be in an unrecorded hand, exactly, and GameState.hand_deal_problems enforces the arithmetic both ways. It also turned a redundant input into a necessary one — with every hand recorded, availability is just 5 - shares_owned (rules p.2), so there is nothing to type.

Two consequences worth recording, both settled by the rulebook rather than by preference. beliefs.secret_pool had to start counting the unrecorded shares: they are as public as any other — missing from the stock beside the board is what makes them known to exist — so leaving them out shrank the pool below the unknown slots it prices and made every unidentified share come out too cheap. And the identities surface in exactly one place. Taking credit isn't it: an encumbered share is set aside face-down (p.8), so the picker just offers "an unrecorded share". Game end is, since each player "counts his cash and adds the value of his shares" — so scoring reveals every hidden hand first, limited to wares the stock left outstanding.

See Manilla Handoff for the full feature list and the exact numbers in play.

4ev-math

Probability / EV layer

● Shipped

Exact (non-simulated) probability and expected-coin-return math for every dice-driven placement decision, split across two modules, both pure functions with no Tkinter import and full Fraction-exact arithmetic:

  • manilla/engine/probability.py — movement_distribution(rounds) (exact pip-sum distribution over N die rolls, plus a per-ware wrapper) and position_outcomes(start, rounds_remaining), resolving a punt's exact arrived / caught_on_13 / shipwrecked probabilities, honoring the mid-voyage overshoot-arrival rule.
  • manilla/engine/expected_value.py — ware_slot_ev (accounts for how many accomplices split the cargo profit and an optional pirate-safety estimate), pirate_slot_ev (sums plunder EV across every loaded punt — plunder is automatic once a punt is caught, so no opponent-decision uncertainty applies), and dock_slot_ev for port/shipyard (an exact Poisson-binomial fill-count distribution, since a slot fills in strict arrival order and so only depends on how many punts arrive, not which one).

32 new unit tests (53 total). Pirate presence on a punt caught on 13 is the one place an opponent decision enters: every EV function that touches it takes p_safe_if_caught as an explicit parameter rather than assuming a value.

5regression

Learned share-buying policy

◐ Partial

The model shipped 2026-08-30 as manilla/engine/share_model.py: a ridge regression solved through the normal equations, no external library, predicting each ware's final black-market level. A share is worth what its ware is worth when the game ends, so predicting that and subtracting today's price turns four predictions into one decision.

Retrained on REV rows and wired in live, 2026-08-31. Both callers — selfplay._run_buy_share and BoardSetupApp._bot_buy_share — now buy through the model, and bidding prices the bid off the same planned purchase, so the two can no longer name different wares. What remains for this milestone is the follow-up work in the list below, not the model itself.

Scored in pesos rather than ranking accuracy (2026-08-31), because "did the highest-predicted ware finish highest" isn't the decision the model makes — the real rule maximises predicted level minus price, only over wares with shares left, and may decline entirely. On held-out REV games:

That reframes the problem twice over. Random choice already earns +10.4 and the model never declines, so the question isn't whether to buy but which. And it compresses the apparent margin honestly: ranking accuracy showed 54.4% against the heuristic's 42.9%, but in pesos the gap is +3.4 on a base where random already captures most of the available value.

  • The first version lost to a one-line heuristic, and that was the useful part. Built on exactly the originally-specified features — share standings plus the favoured punts — it scored R² 0.086 and picked the best ware 41.2% of the time, against 43.0% for simply always buying the favoured ware. The missing input was each ware's current black-market level, which bounds the final one from below: a ware at 20 needs one more rise, a ware at 0 needs four. It wasn't in the recorded schema at all. Adding it moved R² to 0.350 and top-pick accuracy to 60.6% against the same 37% heuristic, on identical game-wise splits.
  • That also exposed a misleading coefficient worth remembering before reading anything into this model: shares_in_play looked genuinely useful at +0.63 — until the market level was present, at which point it collapsed to about -0.03. It had only ever been proxying for the level.
  • Splits are by game, never by row. Every voyage in a game carries the same label (that game's final black market), so a row-wise split would put voyage 3 in training and voyage 4 in test and score well by recognising the game rather than learning anything.
  • Retrained 2026-09-03 on 31,330 voyages pooling four- and five-player games: R² 0.229, +13.70 pesos per voyage against +10.76 for the heuristic and a +19.95 oracle.
  • Pooling the two player counts was checked, not assumed — and it points the opposite way to the seat values. Nothing in the model takes player count as a feature, so the question was whether ware value behaves the same in both. Trained on one and tested on the other: R² 0.227 (4p→5p) and 0.228 (5p→4p), against 0.254 and 0.235 native — and a five-player-trained model actually scores better on four-player games by net value (+14.06 vs +13.91) than the four-player-native one. Ware value transfers across player counts; seat value emphatically does not. Two findings on the same axis, opposite conclusions, which is exactly why each needed its own test rather than an analogy from the other.
  • Still open: favored_wares records only the top two of the three loaded wares, so the model can't distinguish the third loaded ware from the one left ashore. And the shipped coefficients are 4-player; a 5-player run is under way. The residual regret is 5.5 pesos per voyage, and the worked examples suggest where some of it lives — the model leans on current level, so it favours wares that have already climbed, while ginseng repeatedly won from zero. Momentum isn't in the features at all.
  • Speed reality check, measured: a random-policy game is nearly free (3,000 full 4-player games in 8s), while a REV game costs ~31s. That gap is why the structural question above could be settled in seconds on random data while the REV retrain needs a real run.
  • Scope narrowed 2026-08-29: the regression covers share buying only — not first_mover_value, and not harbor-master bidding as a whole. The bidding decision keeps its existing hand-tuned components; only the share-buying half moves to a learned model. The per-seat positional advantage that first_mover_value is trying to price is answered directly by harbor_master_profit.jsonl's plain per-offset means (see milestone 7) — with only 4–5 seat offsets there's no dimensionality problem a regression would solve, and the measured shape is a step (harbor master clearly ahead, the rest near-flat) rather than the smooth per-position slope a fitted coefficient would imply.
  • Also staying off the learned path: pirate placement and ware/dock EV keep the exact analytical treatment (expected_value.py's dice-probability math) — learning those from noisy self-play samples would only add approximation error on top of numbers that are already exact.
  • Regression, not classification, even for skewed/high-variance targets: share-buying value is modeled as a plain expected-value target, consistent with how REV already treats every other decision — no risk-sensitivity term planned.
  • Given the project's zero-dependency convention, default plan is a hand-rolled regression (e.g. closed-form linear/ridge regression) rather than a real ML library, unless that's revisited later.
  • Feature design is still open — raw numeric state features (share counts per ware, cash, positions, etc.) fed directly to the model, no need to canonicalize or enumerate the state space first.
  • Training-data collection itself shipped 2026-08-28: manilla/engine/selfplay_data.py's record_self_play_games hooks into run_voyage/run_game's new optional on_loaded/on_voyage_end callbacks and appends bid_buy_training.jsonl — one row per voyage: shares currently owned per ware and which 2 of the 3 loaded wares got the most favorable (highest, i.e. shortest-remaining-distance) start position, labeled with that game's eventual final black-market standings once the game actually ends (every voyage in a game shares the one label its game converged to). JSON Lines, not a single JSON array or a SQL database — appendable per-game without rewriting the file, crash-safe mid-run, streams without loading everything into memory, and matches the project's existing zero-dependency json-only convention.
6ecr-agent

Heuristic ECR agent

● Shipped

An action-selection policy — formalized as Relative Expected Value (REV) — that scores a candidate action by its ripple effect across every rival's wealth, not just the acting player's own. Fully wired into live bot decisions now, selectable per seat (Human / Computer (random) / Computer (REV)) via a single seat-type selector, with a one-click "Simulate (all REV computers)" to watch four of them play a whole voyage unattended:

  • manilla/engine/beliefs.py — infers hidden opponent share identities from public signals (a harbor-master purchase, a punt started 4+ spaces forward), pooling everything still unconfirmed and averaging it uniformly, weighted by black-market price.
  • manilla/engine/wealth.py — wealth_estimate (every opponent's figure padded +15 pesos, a defensive margin against what beliefs can't see), action_impact (simulates a candidate action on a cloned board and reports its ripple across every current rival), plus lookahead scorers for pilot moves and the pirate captain's free mid-voyage boarding.
  • manilla/engine/harbor_master.py — the auction/bidding decision, three components summed: share-buying value (coupled to the harbor master's own punt-loading choice — a ware's chance of rising this voyage is 0 unless that choice actually loads it), punt-setup value (exact dice probabilities, direct cargo payout plus indirect black-market-rise effect), and first-mover value (as of 2026-08-30, priced from measured per-seat earnings via manilla/engine/seat_value.py rather than the random 0.5–2.0 coefficient it used to take — see milestone 7). Bidding also prices two liquidity risks that don't stack — 3 pesos if a bid would drop cash below 10, or, if it applies, 15 pesos per encumbered share at real risk of the game ending before it can be freed.
  • manilla/engine/policy.py — choose_accomplice_action picks the best port/shipyard/ware/pirate/pilot/insurance placement each turn, scored on the same REV scale as the bidding decision.

Verified end-to-end with a headless Tkinter test that runs a full 4-bot REV voyage through the real UI, not just the engine's own unit tests. Hardened through several rounds of user-reported bugs and corrections since shipping — a stalled auction and an EV-blind accomplice policy (both traced to the same missing REV baseline), a mis-clickable auction turn, a seat-picker trap that silently left "computer" seats un-set, a profit notification missing pirate/insurance payouts, an occupancy projection that stayed optimistic too long into the final round, a displayed pirate EV missing its captain-only boarding bonus, a dock-slot EV that ignored the pirate captain's real port-vs-shipyard choice, and — found 2026-08-28 while auditing pilot behavior — the live pilot-phase decision itself never actually consulted wealth.py's best_pilot_move lookahead at all: _bot_small_pilot_move/_bot_large_pilot_move picked randomly regardless of policy, so REV bots were piloting exactly like random ones. Fixed with a new best_pilot_move_spec/pilot_move_specs pair in wealth.py (reports the winning move as plain (punt_id, delta) data instead of an opaque mutator, so the live game can replay it through its own dock-slot-aware move method), wired into both BoardSetupApp and selfplay.py. Verified against two hand-built scenarios: pulling two punts back from space 13 so a pirate-pilot can still catch a roll of 1 (staying at 13 is a guaranteed safe overshoot next roll — 0% catch chance), and pushing a solely-held punt forward to 13 to escape a rival's pirates. 434 tests across the whole suite.

7tourney

4-bot tournament & analysis

◐ Partial

The headless batch runner this milestone needed now exists (manilla.engine.selfplay.run_self_play_games, shipped 2026-08-28 alongside milestone 5's prerequisite work) — many games, no Tk window, deterministic from a seed. What's still missing is actually running it at scale and comparing REV against random statistically (win rate, standings distribution) rather than just observing "it completes." The old UI-driven "Simulate" button still exists for watching one game unattended, but batch analysis should go through run_self_play_games instead.

manilla/engine/selfplay_data.py's record_self_play_games (shipped 2026-08-28, see milestone 5) also writes harbor_master_profit.jsonl — one row per voyage, keyed by seat offset from the harbor master. Deliberately not a regression target: the per-position advantage is a marginal question about a distribution, and a plain mean per offset answers it. This is the number first_mover_value's hand-picked 0.5–2.0 coefficient is currently guessing at.

Corrected 2026-08-29 — an earlier reading of this data was measuring the wrong thing. The cash snapshot was originally taken at the very top of the voyage, before the auction, so each figure was accomplice earnings minus the winning bid and share purchase. That's what produced the earlier claim that the harbor master came out net negative: the office cost 16–25 pesos per voyage and all of it was being charged against offset 0. The snapshot now happens after the auction and share purchase are both settled (run_voyage's cash_after_setup), isolating what a seat's accomplices actually earned, and a regression test pins it there. Keeping the price out is what makes the data usable at all for milestone 5: a model trained on a figure its own bids had already moved would be fitting a circular target.

Settled 2026-09-02 after three successive runs, the last of which corrected a measurement error that had been inflating the headline number all along. 14,798 four-player REV voyages across 2,234 games, standard error 0.08–0.09 clustered by game:

  • An encumbrance is a loan, not income — and getting that wrong cost 7.5 pesos on one seat. Earnings were a plain cash diff, so a player who ran short and pledged a share gained SHARE_LOAN_AMOUNT and read as 12 pesos richer. Correcting it (schema v3) dropped offset 0 by 7.53 while no other seat moved more than 0.44. The asymmetry is the mechanism: paying for the auction and the share is what makes anyone run short, so the harbor master collected phantom income nearly every voyage and nobody else did.
  • "It's a step, not a slope" was true of four players only — and was recorded here as if it were general. At four it is a step: the harbor master ~3.4 ahead, the rest within ~1.4 of each other, seat 3 even beating seat 2. Measuring five players on 2026-09-03 (12,935 voyages, se 0.07–0.08) gave 7.837 / 4.136 / 3.348 / 2.888 / 2.223 — a clean monotonic decline across every seat, the last earning barely a quarter of the harbor master's. Same game, same policy, different shape. Reading a table per player count is what makes first_mover_value indifferent to which regime it's in; fitting any curve would have baked in whichever count happened to be measured first.
  • The fallback for unmeasured player counts is weaker than it looked. It had predicted 9.506 / 5.314 / 5.314 / 5.314 / 5.314 for five players against the actual figures above — wrong in level and in shape. Only 3-player remains unmeasured, and it is now documented as a placeholder rather than an estimate.
  • Plausible mechanism for the last-seat bump, still untested: the rotation restarts at the harbor master each round, so the last seat always places knowing every other placement, and in the final round the occupancy projection switches to actual occupancy — the last placer knows exactly what they're getting.
  • Worth noting how the estimates moved. The original hand-picked coefficient valued the office at ~3.75 mid-range. The first measurement said 10.5–12.0 and was reported as evidence REV had been badly under-bidding. The corrected figure is 3.4–4.8 — much closer to the original guess than to the first measurement. The guess wasn't what was wrong; the measurement was.
  • Answered: whether feeding these back into bidding shifts them. It doesn't — re-measuring after bidding switched to them moved no seat by more than 0.41, because the placement policy never reads the seat table. The channel that did matter was the encumbrance one above, which is about budget rather than policy.
  • Caveat that still stands: this measures cash only. The share the harbor master buys and their control over punt positioning never touch this delta, so it understates the role.

Recommended next step

The share model is trained, wired in, and scored; seat values are measured and corrected. What's left:

  1. 3-player seat values. Four and five players are now measured (2026-09-02 and 2026-09-03). Three is the last unmeasured count, and since the two measured ones disagree about the shape entirely, its fallback is a placeholder rather than an estimate — worth a run rather than a guess.
  2. Milestone 7, batch comparison. Still outstanding, and the last genuinely open question: whether, and by how much, REV beats random on win rate and final standings. Everything measured so far compares seats within a policy, never the policies against each other.
  3. Close the share model's remaining regret. 5.5 pesos per voyage against perfect foresight, with momentum the obvious missing feature — a ware that has already climbed looks attractive to the model, but the ware that runs to 30 often starts at 0.

A pattern worth carrying forward: of the last five substantive findings here, four concerned where a number came from rather than what was done with it — three measurement errors, and one generalisation that held for only one player count — a snapshot taken at the wrong moment, a missing feature, and a loan counted as income. Each was invisible in the test suite, which passed identically before and after every one of them. Where a number comes from has been consistently more dangerous than what's done with it.
